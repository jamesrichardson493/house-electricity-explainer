First-Principles Guide

5 Things Most Explanations Get Wrong

  • Electrons don't race from the power plant. In 60 Hz AC copper wire, each free electron merely jiggles back and forth across a fraction of a micron (~0.1 mm/s peak drift).
  • Energy flows in the empty space outside the wire. The copper guides the electric (E) and magnetic (B) fields; the energy itself travels at near light speed in the Poynting flux (S = E × B) wrapping the wire.
  • AC still delivers one-way energy. When voltage reverses each half-cycle, both E and B flip sign—so (-E) × (-B) still points forward into your lamp!
  • Neutral is not "dead"—and Ground is for safety, not normal return. Neutral carries full return current back to the transformer's center tap. Ground carries zero current unless a metal fault occurs.
  • Heat & light come from atomic scattering. As the E-field nudges free electrons, they collide with vibrating copper/tungsten ions, turning field energy into lattice vibrations and photons.

Practical US Parallel Wiring Checklist (NEC Essentials)

  • What "Parallel" Means (vs. Series): Hot (120 V) and Neutral (0 V) act as two continuous ladder rails. Each outlet bridges across both rails independently—so every outlet gets full 120 V, currents add (I_total = I₁ + I₂), and unplugging one device never kills downstream outlets.
  • Kill & Test Dead: Flip the branch breaker OFF and verify 0 V on the existing outlet with a Non-Contact Voltage Tester before touching any wire.
  • Intact Break-Off Tabs vs. Pigtails: Keep the Silver & Brass side tabs intact on Outlet #1 to feed Outlet #2 in parallel (or splice pigtails). For Ground, always twist incoming, outgoing, and pigtail inside a green wire nut so only one wire lands on the green screw.
  • Match Gauge & Color Code: Use 14 AWG on 15 A or 12 AWG on 20 A circuits. Bare/Green → Green; White → Silver (wide slot); Black → Brass (narrow slot), using clockwise J-hooks.
  • Safety & GFCI: Kitchens, baths, garages, and outdoor outlets require GFCI protection. Consult local code or a licensed electrician when unsure.
